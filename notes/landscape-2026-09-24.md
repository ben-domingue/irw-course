# Landscape analysis: what first psychometrics courses cover (2026-09-24)

A research agent produced this for the lesson-list review. It compares the draft
29-lesson list with 11 syllabi read in full, 3 read from their descriptions only
("+d" below), 6 general intro texts, 2 IRT texts, the AERA/APA/NCME Standards
(2014) and the NCME ITEMS modules. The sample leans toward US psychology and
education courses and toward Furr. Crocker & Algina's chapter-level coding was
not verified against the book.

## Gaps
1. **Factor analysis, EFA then CFA.** EFA is in 8/11 syllabi and 6/6 texts, the
   most common missing topic; CFA is in 6/11 and 4/6. Suggested lessons: FA I
   (polychorics, parallel analysis, rotation, loadings; CTT module) and FA II (CFA,
   fit, ordinal CFA as the 2PL/GRM, omega; a bridge into IRT). IRW has many
   Likert multi-scale inventories.
2. **Generalizability theory.** 5/11 syllabi (+d2), 5/6 texts, and the
   Standards. Suggested lesson: variance components, G and D studies. IRW has a
   `rater_mediated` collection (a search for "rater" returned 37 tables) and ESM
   tables for occasion facets.
3. **Building an instrument.** Construction is in 9/11 syllabi and item writing
   in 7/11; most courses end with an instrument project. Suggested lesson: from
   construct map to items (blueprint, item-writing rules, cognitive interviews,
   pilot, classical item analysis). IRW item text allows critique of real items;
   it carries no reuse licence.
4. **Scores and their meaning.** Norms are in ~6/11 syllabi and 4/6 texts;
   standard setting is in 1/11 (+d1). Suggested lesson: percentiles, z/T scores,
   norm samples, SEM bands, a short treatment of standard setting, score reports.
5. **Validity evidence in practice.** 5–6/11 syllabi. Convergent/discriminant,
   MTMM, criterion validity, range restriction, classification accuracy. The
   draft's validity lessons are theory only.
6. **Response styles and careless responding.** 5/11 syllabi and ITEMS 32.
   Acquiescence, extreme responding, reverse keying, social desirability, low
   effort. It motivates IRTrees later.
7. **Fairness beyond DIF, ethics and test use.** 7/11 syllabi. Predictive bias
   (the Cleary model), the Standards, accommodations, the history of testing.
8. **Fold into existing lessons rather than add:** omega; SEM, confidence bands
   and difference scores; interrater reliability (into G-theory); multigroup-CFA
   invariance (a section of DIF).

## Unusual for a first course (candidates to mark optional)
Typical courses spend 1–3 weeks on IRT; the draft has 9 core IRT lessons plus 8
in "Beyond". Candidates: estimating item parameters; parameter invariance;
guessing and priors; the 4PL part of 1PL→4PL; model fit and prediction;
dimensionality/MIRT (once FA exists); item banks/CAT; linking/equating
(borderline); invariance under treatment; Borsboom's validity (as an optional
pair to Kane/Messick); all of "Beyond" except polytomous. Recommendation: promote
**polytomous models** into the core, because most IRW data is Likert and the FA
and response-style lessons depend on it.

## Recommended additions, in priority order
1. Factor analysis I: EFA and dimensionality
2. Factor analysis II: CFA, omega, and IRT as factor analysis
3. From construct map to items: design, item writing, classical item analysis
4. Generalizability theory
5. Scores and their meaning: norms, SEM bands, standard setting, reporting
6. Gathering validity evidence: convergent/discriminant, criterion, classification
7. Response styles and careless responding
8. Fair tests, fair uses: predictive bias, Standards, history, ethics

Adding 1–6, marking ~10 current lessons optional and promoting polytomous models
gives about 20–22 core lessons.
