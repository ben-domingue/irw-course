# Missing responses: omitted, not reached, and ignorable (`missing-responses`)

Module: irt · Prereqs: ability-estimation · Extension · Status: stub (outlined)

## Core ideas

1. **Why a response is missing is part of the data.** Rubin's three mechanisms, stated for item responses: MCAR (a random skip), MAR (missingness depends only on what was observed: a booklet design, an adaptive test, a stopping rule), MNAR (it depends on what wasn't observed: the response itself, or $\theta$). A missingness indicator $m_{ij}$ sits beside $x_{ij}$. The long table can't tell these apart; only the design and the codes can. *(major)* Sources: Rubin (1976), doi:10.1093/biomet/63.3.581; Little & Rubin (2019), doi:10.1002/9781119482260; Seaman, Galati, Jackson & Carlin (2013), doi:10.1214/13-STS415; Schafer & Graham (2002), doi:10.1037/1082-989X.7.2.147.
2. **Ignorable: when MML can leave the gaps alone.** If the mechanism is MAR and its parameters are distinct from the model's, the likelihood of the observed responses is the right likelihood. So `mirt`'s marginal ML uses every observed response and needs no complete cases. That is why booklets, adaptive tests and concurrent calibration work, even though an adaptive test's missingness depends on ability through the earlier answers. Listwise deletion is valid under MCAR only, and in a booklet design it keeps nobody: TIMSS 2007 has 0 complete cases. An "if you've done `fa-confirmatory`" Recall covers FIML in `lavaan`, the same idea (E2). *(major)* Sources: Rubin (1976); Mislevy & Wu (1996), doi:10.1002/j.2333-8504.1996.tb01708.x; Mislevy (2017, Handbook Vol. 2), doi:10.1201/b19166-10; Bock & Aitkin (1981), doi:10.1007/BF02293801; Chalmers (2012), doi:10.18637/jss.v048.i06; Enders & Bandalos (2001), doi:10.1207/S15328007SEM0803_5; Finch (2008), doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.2008.00062.x.
3. **Omitted: wrong, missing, or something in between?** Scoring an omit as wrong assumes the respondent would have got it wrong. Leaving it missing assumes the omit says nothing about $\theta$. Lord argued that omits can't properly be scored as wrong, and proposed a way to use the information they carry. Where low scorers omit more, leaving omits missing moves them *up*, and EAP shrinkage adds to the move (Recall of `ability-estimation`). Scoring them wrong moves them down. The question the data can answer: does omission track ability? *(major)* Sources: Lord (1974), doi:10.1007/BF02291471 (his specific method, reportedly credit at the chance level, is to be checked in the paper when drafting); De Ayala, Plake & Impara (2001), doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.2001.tb01124.x; Robitzsch (2021), doi:10.3390/ejihpe11040117.
4. **Model the propensity to respond.** In the latent-ignorability models, a second latent variable (response propensity $\xi_j$) drives the $m_{ij}$ and correlates with $\theta$. The missingness is then ignorable given both latent variables, and the correlation carries the information. The Mislevy–Wu model lets an omit depend on the item itself; scoring omits as wrong and latent ignorability are both special cases of it. The question: how far do these models move the estimates in real data? In the NEPS competence tests, not much (Pohl et al.). An "if you've done `irtrees`" Recall covers the skip node, which irtrees showed in simulation only (E2). Sources: Holman & Glas (2005), doi:10.1348/000711005X47168; Rose, von Davier & Xu (2010), doi:10.1002/j.2333-8504.2010.tb02218.x; Rose, von Davier & Nagengast (2017), doi:10.1007/s11336-016-9544-7; Pohl, Gräfe & Rose (2014), doi:10.1177/0013164413504926; Köhler, Pohl & Carstensen (2015), doi:10.1177/0013164414561785; Debeer, Janssen & De Boeck (2017), doi:10.1111/jedm.12147.
5. **Not reached: time, not knowledge.** A not-reached item is one at the end of a part that the respondent never got to. TIMSS treats not-reached items as not administered when it calibrates the items, and as incorrect when it scores students. Scored wrong in calibration, end-of-test items look harder than they are. Not-reached items tell us about speed, which may or may not go with ability, and the rest of speededness belongs with time limits. The IEA's definition is itself a rule about order: an unanswered item is not reached when the item before it is also unanswered and nothing later in that part is answered. So where the order of presentation is known, the codes can be recovered from the gaps alone (Data audit 4). *(major)* Sources: Foy, Galia & Li (2008), TIMSS 2007 Technical Report ch. 11, §11.3.3 (verified in the publisher's PDF, timssandpirls.bc.edu/TIMSS2007/PDF/T07_TR_Chapter11.pdf); van der Linden (2018), "Test speededness and time limits", Handbook Vol. 3, ch. 12, pp. 249–266, doi:10.1201/9781315117430-12 (publisher's page; not registered on Crossref); Oshima (1994), doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.1994.tb00443.x; Bolt, Cohen & Wollack (2002), doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.2002.tb01146.x; Glas & Pimentel (2008), doi:10.1177/0013164408315262.

**Verdict (proposed, soft and impersonal):**
- If omissions are rare and unrelated to ability, leaving them missing under MML may be sufficient for some purposes.
- If omission tracks ability, as it does in PIRLS 2023 South Africa (13% of items omitted in the lowest fifth by plausible value, 7% in the highest) and in TIMSS 2007 Russia (14% against 4%), neither scoring rule is neutral. Reporting $\theta$ both ways, with a response-propensity model as a check, may then be sufficient for some purposes.
- For not-reached items, the TIMSS rule (missing when calibrating, wrong when scoring) separates what an item is from what a respondent did. It is one defensible choice among several.

**1. How the IRW represents a missing response.** The standard (v1.0 beta, 2026-09-19, clause C4):
- A code for a missing or non-substantive answer (−9, 99, "don't know") is not a response. Rows without a response SHOULD be omitted.
- `resp_raw` may carry the source's own code for an omitted response beside a scored `resp`.
- No column says *why* a response is missing: omitted, not reached and not administered look the same. A missing response is an absent row or, in older tables, a row with `resp` empty (C4 lists the text NA as the most common defect).

**4. Trailing runs: inferring not-reached from order.** On a test given in a fixed order, a run of unanswered items after the respondent's last answer is the signature of not reached; a gap before the last answer is an omit. The IRW keeps the gaps (as absent rows), so this needs only the order of presentation and where each timed part ends. The rule used throughout is the IEA's: within a part, an unanswered item is not reached if the item before it is also unanswered and nothing after it is answered. So the first item of a trailing run counts as omitted, and a run of one is an omit.

*Does the rule recover the true codes?* Two sources keep both codes.
- **PIRLS 2023, South Africa** (the source of `mthimkhulu_2023_pirls_reading`): one released passage, 15 items numbered in order, 1,894 learners in booklets 12 and 17. Applied to the IRW table alone (absent rows, item order from the item names, the passage as one part), the rule reproduces the source codes exactly: 4,032 of 4,032 not reached and 3,107 of 3,107 omitted. The 17 system-missing cells come out as 11 not reached and 6 omitted.
- **TIMSS 2007, Russia** (`sirt::data.timss07.G8.RUS`): 30,333 omitted or not-reached cells. The booklet can be recovered from which blocks a student saw (14 booklets of four blocks; the file's column order within each block matches the booklet order), and the TIMSS 2007 booklet design says which two blocks make each part. With the parts known, the rule agrees with the IEA codes on 99.6% of cells (3,804 of 3,811 not reached recovered; 123 omits called not reached). Three simplifications do worse: calling the whole trailing run not reached (93.3%; 2,023 omits misread), ignoring the break between parts (92.2%; only 39% of not-reached cells found), and treating each block as its own part (95.8%). So the rule works when the order *and the part boundaries* are known, and it is sensitive to both.

*Candidates scanned.*
- **`mthimkhulu_2023_pirls_reading`: the clearest case.** Tokenless CSV, CC BY 4.0. 25% of cells are missing. The not-reached share climbs along the passage, from 0 on the first two items to 35% on the last; omits stay between 5% and 19% throughout. 35% of learners have at least one not-reached item, and 30% have a trailing run of three or more. With plausible value 1 as the ability measure, omits track ability and not-reached items don't: r = −0.16 against −0.02. By fifth of ability, omits fall from 13% to 7% of items, while not reached runs 11%, 15%, 17%, 18%, 11%. Rasch (partial credit) fits in `mirt`: scoring every gap wrong moves the last five items' mean location up 0.40 logits relative to the IEA rule, and the first five up 0.06. The 427 learners with five or more not-reached items average −0.51 SD with gaps scored wrong, −0.10 left missing and −0.20 under the rule. Two points to check: the multiple-choice items have more code 9 than the constructed-response items (17% against 9%), perhaps because 9 also means "invalid" (a double mark); and the table's description says English and isiZulu while the source records 11 test languages.
- **`pirlsmissing_sirt`** (PIRLS 2011, codes merged in the source): two passages, each assumed to be its own part (to be checked against the PIRLS 2011 design). 20% of the missing cells are in trailing runs; 8.1% of students have a run of three or more (2.8% in passage G, 6.6% in P). Here both kinds track the sum score on the answered items: trailing length r = −0.15, mid-passage gaps r = −0.25. A run of three or more falls from 14% of students in the lowest fifth to 2.4% in the highest.
- **ENEM (`enem_2022_1mil_mt_nom`, `_ch_nom`).** Order is fully recoverable: `position` is the item's place in the candidate's own 180-item booklet, and `booklet` is the form. The four colour forms order the same 45 items differently (42 of 44 mathematics items sit at four different positions), and two small adapted forms (about 24,000 candidates) add a fifth. Mathematics ends the second day (positions 136–180) and human sciences the first (46–90). But blanks are rare: 0.27% of mathematics cells (4.4% of candidates have any), and only 0.13% of candidates end mathematics with a run of three or more blanks (0.48% in human sciences). Trailing length is unrelated to the proportion correct on the answered items (r = 0.01 and −0.01). A blank earns what a wrong answer earns, so a candidate who runs out of time has reason to fill in the rest. What does show up is straight-lining: 0.29% of candidates end mathematics with eight or more identical letters, more than a hundred times what independent choices would give (about 0.002%), and their proportion correct on the last ten items is 0.22, near chance. That is a lesson about rapid guessing (`careless-responding`) more than about missingness. ENEM is also on paper, so the printed position is not necessarily the order of answering. The 45M-row tables can't load in a page.
- **PISA 2015–2022 (`pisa20xx_*`)**: missing responses are absent rows, and there is no booklet, cluster or position column, so the order can't be recovered without the source's booklet design. Not pursued.
- **Other dichotomous tables with density below 1** (13 scanned: `margaretto_2025_*`, `psychtools_ability`, `chess_lnirt`, `roar_gijbels2024`, `test_taking_much_2025_mr`, `preschool_sel_*`, `brand_raffaelli_2024_recognition_20`, `idcr_martinez_2023_*`): none shows a usable trailing-run signature. Most have no gaps once empty respondents are dropped; in `psychtools_ability` the trailing share (18%) is what a random item order would give (17%); the `preschool_sel_*` runs come from the task design.

*What it needs.* Order, and part boundaries: from numbered items within one timed part (PIRLS), from a `position` column (ENEM), or from a published booklet design (TIMSS, PISA). And a test where running out of time leaves a blank rather than a guess.

## Picks up

- EAP shrinks toward the mean, and most for respondents with few responses; the MLE and WLE don't shrink (from `ability-estimation`, thread `shrinkage`). A return is to be added: "shows skippers pulled up when omits are left missing".
- The MLE, its standard error $1/\sqrt{I(\hat\theta)}$, and the posterior (from `ability-estimation`, `information`).
- A respondent who answers only the items they know can have a perfect pattern on those items, and so no MLE (from `ability-estimation`, thread `perfect-patterns`; a possible return).

**The finding:** in PIRLS 2023 (South Africa) and TIMSS 2007 (Russia), omissions carry information about ability and not-reached items carry little, so the two codes deserve different treatment. On a fixed-order test the IRW table alone is enough to tell them apart: the trailing-run rule reproduces the source's codes exactly in PIRLS 2023 and on 99.6% of cells in TIMSS. Where the codes have been merged (PIRLS 2011), the treatment chosen moves a country comparison by nearly a fifth of an SD. And where a blank costs nothing extra (ENEM), running out of time shows up as guessing rather than as gaps.

## Widget / simulation / problem ideas

**Widgets**
- **Three mechanisms.** A grid of 30 respondents × 12 items, shaded by true $\theta$ (reusing `irw-data`'s grid, rewritten locally). Choose MCAR, MAR (booklet or stopping rule) or MNAR (skip if low $\theta$; skip if it would be wrong). Read-outs: the observed item mean against the true one, and the mean $\theta$ of those who answered (idea 1).
- **Ignorable or not.** The same data fitted as missing: the item $b$ recovered against the truth, under each mechanism (idea 2). Carries the optional Core-ideas predict: "an adaptive test's gaps depend on ability; is $b$ biased?"
- **One respondent, omits scored two ways.** Ten Rasch items; tick answered, right and omitted. Likelihood and posterior with omits as wrong vs. missing; MLE, WLE and EAP marked (idea 3; `irt.js` `loglik`).
- **Propensity dial.** The correlation between response propensity and $\theta$ runs from −0.8 to 0. Plot the average error of the heavy skippers' EAP under "wrong", "missing" and the propensity model (idea 4).
- **Read the order.** The `mthimkhulu_2023_pirls_reading` grid in item order (learners sorted by their last answered item). Toggle the rule: every gap an omit, every trailing gap not reached, or the IEA rule (first gap of a trailing run an omit). Read-outs: the counts under each rule and, for the IEA rule, its agreement with the source's codes (100%).
