# Continuous responses: sliders and bounded scales (`continuous-responses`)

Module: beyond · Prereqs: polytomous, fa-confirmatory · Extension · Status: stub (outlined)

## Core ideas

1. **A continuous response is the factor model's latent response, observed.** `fa-confirmatory` cut a latent response $y^* = \lambda\eta + \varepsilon$ at thresholds. A line or a slider hands us $y^*$ (or a monotone function of it) directly. Mellenbergh's linear model is the one-factor model for such items, fit by ML as in `fa-confirmatory`. Its information, $\lambda^2/\psi$ per item, doesn't depend on θ. So the one SEM for everyone comes back. *(major)* Sources: Mellenbergh (1994), doi:10.1207/s15327906mbr2903_2; Mellenbergh (2016), "Models for continuous responses", *Handbook of IRT* Vol. 1, doi:10.1201/9781315374512 (chapter link 10.1201/9781315374512-12 on the publisher's site; **chapter pages unverified**; Crossref has no chapter record); Bejar (1977), doi:10.1177/014662167700100407, an early application to personality items.
2. **The bounds break the linear model, and Samejima's model transforms them away.** A line has ends. A linear model predicts means past them and assumes the same error variance at the ends as in the middle. Samejima's continuous response model (CRM) is the graded model's limit as the categories fill the line. In Wang and Zeng's parameterization it is the linear factor model for the logit $z = \ln\big(x/(M - x)\big)$. On `estcrm_epia`, `factanal` on the logits reproduces `EstCRM`'s item estimates to two decimals: $a$ = slope/residual SD, $\alpha$ = slope, $b$ = −intercept/slope. Information is still constant, because a one-to-one transform of the data leaves Fisher information unchanged. The vignette makes this point, and the lesson cites it. *(major)* Sources: Samejima (1973), doi:10.1007/BF02291114; Wang & Zeng (1998), doi:10.1177/014662169802200402; `EstCRM`: Zopluoglu (2012), doi:10.1177/0146621612436599; Ferrando (2002), doi:10.1207/S15327906MBR3704_05, the source of this lesson's main table, which compares two models for continuous responses (that they are the linear and Samejima models is from the title and the `EstCRM` docs; **to confirm from the paper**).
3. **Models for a bounded response: beta IRT and its relatives.** The response is treated as Beta-distributed given θ, so the bounds are part of the distribution. Under these models information varies with θ, which is the vignette's finding. The lesson treats them briefly: the density widget, plus the vignette's held-out result. Bounded models beat the linear model on held-out log-probability in all 11 of its tables but not on RMSE, so they matter when the question needs the shape of the response and not its mean. Sources: Noel & Dauvier (2007), doi:10.1177/0146621605287691; Müller (1987), a Rasch model for continuous ratings, doi:10.1007/BF02294232; Ferrando (2001), doi:10.1348/000711001159573; IRW vignette `continuous_bounded`.
4. **Responses at the ends are a different kind of response.** The logit of 0 or 1 is infinite, so the CRM needs a squeeze constant, and that constant is a choice that moves the results (`karlsson_2023_climate_risk`: test information 36, 31, 28 as ε goes 0.5, 0.05, 0.005 on the 0–100 scale). Zero-one inflated IRT and ordered beta give the ends their own probabilities. Above a ceiling the scale measures nothing. *(major)* Sources: Smithson & Verkuilen (2006), doi:10.1037/1082-989X.11.1.54; Ospina & Ferrari (2010), doi:10.1007/s00362-008-0125-4; Molenaar, Cúri & Bazán (2022), doi:10.3102/10769986221108455; Kubinec (2023), ordered beta, doi:10.1017/pan.2022.20; `glmmTMB`: Brooks et al. (2017), doi:10.32614/RJ-2017-066.
5. **Discretizing: what it costs, and when it seems to gain.** `polytomous` proved that merging categories can't add information. A continuous response is the limit of that argument. If the continuous model holds, cutting the line costs information, and it costs most in the tails. It can still *gain* against a continuous model whose error assumption is wrong: on the EPI lines the graded model fit to 21 bins reports more information than the linear or Samejima model fit to the raw lines (see Tables). *(major)* Sources: MacCallum, Zhang, Preacher & Rucker (2002), doi:10.1037/1082-989X.7.1.19; Samejima (1973); Lubke & Muthén (2004), doi:10.1207/S15328007SEM1104_2; Rhemtulla et al. (2012, recalled from `fa-confirmatory`), doi:10.1037/a0029315.
6. **Do sliders behave like Likert items with more categories?** This depends on how respondents use the slider. A slider showing numbers can heap on round values, which makes it a scale with a few effective categories. A paper line scored with a ruler can't heap that way. Sources: Couper, Tourangeau, Conrad & Singer (2006), doi:10.1177/0894439305281503; Funke & Reips (2012), doi:10.1177/1525822X12444061; Preston & Colman (2000), doi:10.1016/S0001-6918(99)00050-5; Matejka et al. (2016), doi:10.1145/2858036.2858063; the vignette's quarter-point heaping in `lsbq_maleki_2025_non_persian_proficiency` (cited, not reloaded).

**Proposed verdict (soft, for Ben to confirm):** in the case that responses sit away from the ends and the goal is a score, the linear factor model on the raw responses may be sufficient for some purposes. It and the Samejima model agree closely on the EPI lines (scores correlate 0.96, the same test information of 1.40). In the case that a fifth or more of the responses sit at an end, a model that gives the ends their own probability is the one whose predictions of the ends can be checked against the data. Cutting a slider into five to seven categories may be sufficient for some purposes: it keeps about 90% of the information in the middle, less in the tails.

## Picks up

- $y^* = \lambda\eta + \varepsilon$ and the conversion to slope and difficulty; ordinal FA is the GRM (from `fa-confirmatory`; thread `ordinal-fa-is-grm`). A Recall callout opens idea 1.
- Choosing an estimator from the response distribution: ML for continuous, roughly normal data (from `fa-confirmatory`; thread `estimator-choice`). Here the response is continuous but bounded and piled at the ends.
- The GRM; polytomous information $\sum_k (P_k')^2/P_k$; merging categories can't add information (the Go deeper proof), plus the collapsing widget (from `polytomous`). Recall callout in idea 5.
- Test information and the conditional SEM, $1/\sqrt{I(\theta)}$ (from `information`). The constant SEM of `ctt-reliability` (thread `one-sem`) comes back under the linear and Samejima models.
- Uniqueness $\psi$ and communality (from `fa-exploratory`).
- Brief "if you've done" Recalls (E2, restated, not threads): the lognormal RT model is a linear model for log time, the same structure as Samejima's model with log in place of logit (`response-time`). A beta unfolding model exists for bounded responses (Noel, 2014, doi:10.1007/s11336-013-9361-1) (`unfolding`).

## Widget / simulation / problem ideas

**Widgets** (helpers in `lessons/widgets/continuous-responses.js`)
- Linear vs. Samejima: an item's mean and ±2 SD band against θ. The linear band leaves [0, 1]; the logit band narrows at the ends (ideas 1, 2).
- Densities at one θ: logit-normal, beta, and zero-one inflated beta. Sliders move θ and the inflation shares (ideas 3, 4).
- The squeeze constant: a sample with 20% at the ceiling, its logits for several ε, and the fitted slope. The spike moves as ε shrinks (idea 4).
- Cut the line into K categories: information of the cut item against the continuous item's constant $\lambda^2/\psi$ as K rises, with equal-width or quantile cuts. This extends `polytomous`'s collapsing widget to the limit, and carries the optional Core-ideas predict (idea 5).
- Heaping: a share h of respondents round to the nearest 10 or 25. Shows the histogram and the information left (idea 6).

**Predict-then-check:** "Cut the EPI lines into five categories and fit the graded model. Will its test information at θ = 0 be lower than, about equal to, or higher than the Samejima model's 1.40 on the full lines?" Answer: higher (1.8; 2.0 with 21 bins). The simulation check follows, and then the diagnosis.

**Simulate:** Samejima data (N = 500, 6 items), optionally with a share π at each end and rounding to the nearest 10. Fit `factanal` on raw responses and on logits, and the graded model (`mirt`) on K bins. Compare recovered loadings and information with the truth. Seconds in the browser; `factanal` is base R, and `mirt` uses the course's webR setup.

**Problems**
1. Derivation: show information is $\lambda^2/\psi$ under the linear model, and that it is unchanged by a one-to-one transform of the response. Then show `EstCRM`'s $a$ is slope/residual SD.
2. Real data with a twist (`estcrm_selfeff`, or `karlsson` if the third table is dropped): the Samejima fit with the bound at 11 vs. 10.5 cm (or ε = 0.5 vs. 0.005). What moves, and why?
3. Judgment: a team plans to replace a five-point item with a 0–100 slider "to get more information". What would you check in pilot data (heaping, the ends, reliability by K), and when is the switch worth it?
4. Design: design a slider to limit heaping and piling at the ends. Consider visible numbers, where the handle starts, and the anchors (Couper et al., 2006; Matejka et al., 2016).
5. Real data: `karlsson` wave 2 by arm (`cov_condition`). Fit the ordered beta by condition: does the imagery manipulation move the ends, the middle, or both?
6. Challenge (open): why does the graded model on binned EPI lines report more information than the linear model? Test one explanation: error flatter than normal, variance shrinking near the ends, or a beta response. I don't know which one holds.

## Go deeper

- **Samejima's model is the graded model's limit, and cutting it loses information.** Let the GRM's boundaries fill the line: category probabilities become a density and the information tends to $\lambda^2/\psi$. With `polytomous`'s Cauchy–Schwarz step, any cut version has no more information than the line. Why: pays off `polytomous`'s merging proof, and supports the verdict on discretizing. About half a page.
- *(candidate)* **Fisher information is invariant under a one-to-one transform of the response.** This is why the Samejima model's information is flat although its density on [0, 1] changes shape. Why: here and in problem 1; `response-time` (log time) as an "if you've done" aside. A third of a page. Keep only one of the two if the depth pass says so.
